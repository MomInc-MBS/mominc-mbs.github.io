# HIVE AI — 100 Similar Projects: Succeeded, Failed, or In Progress, and Why

**Scope:** Projects similar to HIVE AI as described in `HIVE_AI_Core_Architecture_0_39e1.md` — modular / composable intelligence, multi-agent orchestration, mixture-of-experts and modular LLMs, specialist and adapter models, cognitive architectures, bio-inspired / neuromorphic / swarm systems, memory / verification / self-improvement, and enterprise orchestration.

**Research date:** 18–19 November 2026. Each project was researched individually against primary sources (official site/docs, GitHub, Wikipedia, company blog, reputable news). Status definitions used throughout:

- **Succeeded** — shipped, widely adopted, commercially or academically sustained, or acquired for its tech and still in use.
- **In Progress** — actively developed / maintained in 2025–2026, with releases, commits, or users.
- **Failed** — shut down, abandoned, pivoted away, or superseded with no active maintenance.
- **Stalled/Abandoned** — no meaningful release, commit, or news in 12+ months, but no formal shutdown.

**Coverage: 100 / 100 researched. No unverified entries.**

## Headline counts

## The pattern — why they succeed or fail

### What the winners have in common

1. **A narrow, measurable job first.** AlphaFold, DeepSeek-Math / Llemma, Med-PaLM / MedGemma, AlphaCode, and the MoE models that shipped (Mixtral, DeepSeek-V3, Qwen-MoE, Llama 4 Maverick) all won on one benchmarkable task before anyone talked about generality. HIVE's Research Question 1 — how small can an expert stay while staying excellent on a precisely defined task — is the right first test.
2. **Deterministic control around the agents, not autonomy alone.** LangGraph (GA 1.0, Oct 2025, no-breaking-changes commitment), CrewAI Flows, Google ADK, and Temporal / Ray survived by giving developers graphs, state, retries, and observability. Pure free-form agent loops — AutoGPT Classic, BabyAGI, AgentGPT — went viral, then stalled or were archived.
3. **A platform or distribution owner.** MCP, OpenAI Agents SDK, Google ADK / A2A, Microsoft Semantic Kernel / Agent Framework, NVIDIA Triton / NIM, and vLLM are in progress or succeeded because a large vendor or the Hugging Face ecosystem keeps them alive. Standalone frameworks without that backing (AutoGen, AgentVerse, OpenAgents) were superseded or went dormant.
4. **Adapters and routing, not retraining everything.** LoRA / PEFT, AdapterHub, Mixture-of-Agents, and LLM routing (FrugalGPT / RouteLLM) succeeded as infrastructure: cheap specialization, swappable skills, and a router that picks the cheapest good-enough expert. That is the closest proven analogue to HIVE's dynamic-specialization layer (Section 5 of the HIVE doc) — and the evidence says it works when the adapters are compatible and trained, not when a role label is just reassigned.
5. **Verification as a first-class layer.** Reflexion, Self-Refine, STaR, and the critic / teacher / reviewer pattern in ChatDev and MetaGPT survived academically and in products because generation without a critic does not compound. HIVE Layer 8 (Verification and Evolution) is not optional garnish; in the winners it is the product.

### Why the failures failed

1. **Coordination overhead ate the gains.** Multi-agent conversation in natural language is slow, lossy, and expensive. Projects that forced every specialist to talk in English, with no domain-native representation or structured Task / Evidence / Control channels, burned tokens and diverged. This is exactly HIVE's Research Question 3 — and it is unproven until measured against a natural-language baseline.
2. **Breaking rewrites fragmented the community.** AutoGen's v0.2 → v0.4 rewrite, and similar re-architectures elsewhere, split users and forks (AG2). A hive that cannot keep a stable orchestration API while specialists change will lose its own users the same way.
3. **Viral demo, no production path.** AutoGPT (188k GitHub stars), AgentGPT (100k+ users in week one), and BabyAGI proved demand for autonomous agents and then had no guardrails, no cost control, no observability, and no enterprise buyer. Stars are not survival.
4. **Hardware without software, or biology as metaphor only.** Loihi, TrueNorth, SpiNNaker, BrainScaleS, and DishBrain are scientifically real and mostly still in progress or niche — but none became a general AI platform, because the programming model and the killer application never arrived together. Bio-inspired coordination (HIVE Layer 6) needs a concrete programming model and a benchmark, not just an analogy.
5. **Cognitive architectures that never shipped a product.** SOAR, ACT-R, CLARION, LIDA, NARS, and OpenCog Hyperon have decades of research and real ideas, but most are stalled or academic-only. The lesson is not that they were wrong — it is that an architecture without a shipped, measured specialist community does not survive.
6. **Consumer hardware bets.** Rabbit R1 and Humane AI Pin failed on the same axis: a specialist device / LAM that could not beat a phone app on reliability, latency, or price. HIVE should not tie its fate to a single device form factor early.

### The uncomfortable middle

- **MoE is the proven modular win, and the cautionary tale.** Switch Transformer, GLaM, Mixtral, DeepSeek-MoE, and Qwen-MoE succeeded as engineering: sparse activation, cheaper inference at scale. But several MoE launches were also superseded within a year by a denser or better-trained model. Modularity alone does not beat a better monolith — HIVE's Research Question 5 (quality / latency / memory / cost vs. strong conventional baselines) has to be answered head-to-head, not assumed.
- **IBM Watson is the classic warning.** A famous demo (Jeopardy, 2011), then Watson Health collapsed on messy real-world data, cost, and overpromising. A hive that claims a domain community before it has a verified specialist in that domain will repeat it.
- **Superseded is not the same as wrong.** AutoGen and Swarm were both 'failed' in this table because their vendors replaced them with a successor (Microsoft Agent Framework, OpenAI Agents SDK). The ideas survived; the brand did not. HIVE should design for that: a stable protocol / memory format outlives any one orchestrator.

## What this means for HIVE — the short version

- Prove one specialist community end-to-end (e.g. mathematics, as in HIVE §4) with a critic, a teacher, and a measured baseline before adding more domains.
- Ship the deterministic layer first: routing, state, retries, ALL-STOP-style control, and observability. The autonomous layer sits on top of it, not instead of it.
- Measure domain-native communication vs. natural-language agent chat on latency, cost, and information loss. If it does not win, cut it.
- Keep adapters compatible and trained; do not assume a 1B core can swap algebra for story by relabelling it.
- Do not build the bio-inspired layer as metaphor. Give it a programming model, a benchmark, and a fallback to ordinary orchestration.

### 1. Microsoft AutoGen — Failed

- **Category:** Multi-agent framework
- **What it is:** Microsoft Research's open-source framework for building multi-agent AI applications in which LLM agents collaborate through structured conversation.
- **Key dates:** GitHub repo created 2023-08-18; first released fall/October 2023; v0.4 ground-up async/event-driven rewrite announced 2025-01-14; last feature release python-v0.7.5 on 2025-09-30; maintenance mode from 2025-10-02; successor Microsoft Agent Framework 1.0 GA 2026-04-03; AutoGen README changed to 'archived and no longer maintained' on 2026-10-16 per secondary reports, confirmed in the live README fetched 2026-11-19.

- **Why:** AutoGen succeeded as research and popularised conversational multi-agent orchestration, but its v0.2-to-v0.4 breaking rewrite fragmented users and its original creators left to continue the old design as the AG2 fork. Microsoft then consolidated AutoGen with Semantic Kernel into a single enterprise-supported Microsoft Agent Framework with stable APIs and long-term support, so the standalone project was superseded by design rather than collapsing from a single technical failure.
- **Relevance to HIVE:** Copy AutoGen's conversation/group-chat orchestration and layered Core/AgentChat/Extensions separation, but avoid a breaking rewrite, single-vendor consolidation risk, and dependence on a framework without a stable API and migration path.

- **Relevance to HIVE:** Copy its lesson that the simplest possible routing (top-1, one expert per token) beats cleverer multi-expert schemes on stability, communication cost, and scaling — but avoid assuming experts specialize semantically, since later analyses show routing correlates more with syntax/position than topic.

- **Category:** Multi-agent framework
- **What it is:** A layered inference-time method in which multiple LLM proposers independently answer a prompt and aggregator LLMs synthesize their outputs into a single improved response, with no fine-tuning.

The original repo (github.com/ezelikman/STaR, created 2022-10-03) is frozen at just 6 commits, 236 stars, 27 forks, 0 releases — but the method is heavily cited and lives on in successors: V-STaR (2024), Quiet-STaR (2024), STaR-GATE and CARE-STaR (2025), and its generate-filter-finetune loop is now a standard ingredient in RLVR pipelines behind DeepSeek-R1/o1-style reasoning models.
- **Why:** Technically it worked and was simple: it needed only a few seed rationales plus questions with known answers, and it demonstrably closed most of the gap to a 30x larger model. Its limits — dependence on ground-truth labels, need for initial above-chance reasoning ability, and rationalization that can reverse-engineer answers without true reasoning — meant the standalone JAX/TPU codebase froze after 2022 while the idea was absorbed and superseded by verifier-augmented variants (V-STaR, Quiet-STaR) and large-scale RLVR/GRPO training. It succeeded as a foundational technique rather than as a maintained product.
- **Relevance to HIVE:** Copy STaR's grounded self-improvement loop — generate attempts, keep only those verified against ground truth, retrain, repeat — but add a verifier/process check, since answer-only filtering lets spurious rationales slip through.

- **Relevance to HIVE:** Copy Rabbit's teach-mode idea of learning actions from demonstrated workflows, but avoid demo-first dedicated hardware and general-agent claims — ship the agent as software on devices users already own and prove reliability on narrow tasks first.
- **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabbit_r1; https://www.wired.com/story/rabbit-r1-os3-jesse-lyu/; https://heytalaya.com/2026/10/humane-ai-pin-rabbit-r1-what-happened/

### 94. Humane AI Pin — Failed

- **Category:** Orchestration/Enterprise
- **What it is:** A screenless, voice-controlled wearable AI pin intended as a smartphone replacement, powered by Humane's cloud-based CosmOS platform.
- **Key dates:** Founded 2018; AI Pin unveiled 9 Nov 2023; shipped Apr 2024; HP asset deal announced 18 Feb 2025; AI Pin discontinued and servers shut down 28 Feb 2025
- **Evidence:** Raised about $230M; launched at $699 plus $24/month, cut to $499 in Oct 2024; only about 10,000 units shipped (~7,000 still in customers' hands, ~$9M revenue) with returns at times outpacing sales; HP acquired CosmOS, 300+ patents/applications and most staff for $116M on 18 Feb 2025, and Humane shut down AI Pin servers at noon PT on 28 Feb 2025, bricking core calling, messaging and AI functions.

### 96. Google A2A Protocol (Agent2Agent) + ADK ecosystem — In Progress

- **Category:** Multi-agent framework
- **What it is:** Google's open, vendor-neutral Agent2Agent (A2A) protocol for agents built on different frameworks to discover each other and delegate tasks, plus the Agent Development Kit (ADK), Google's open-source multi-agent SDK in Python, TypeScript, Go, Java and Kotlin.
- **Key dates:** 9 Apr 2025: Google announces A2A and ADK at Cloud Next, with 50+ A2A partners; May 2025: ADK Python 1.0; 23 Jun 2025: A2A donated to Linux Foundation; Aug 2025: IBM Agent Communication Protocol merges into A2A; 12 Mar 2026: A2A v1.0.0; 9 Apr 2026: 150+ organisations reported; 19 May 2026: ADK Python 2.0 GA; 27 Aug 2026: A2A joins Agentic AI Foundation as Growth Stage project; 12 Nov 2026: ADK Python 2.14.1

### 97. OpenAI ChatGPT / GPT-4o monolith baseline — Succeeded

- **Category:** MoE/Modular LLM
- **What it is:** A general-purpose, single-model conversational AI product — ChatGPT powered by the natively multimodal GPT-4o flagship model — used as the monolithic control against multi-agent and modular systems.
- **Key dates:** ChatGPT launched 30 Nov 2022; GPT-4o launched 13 May 2024; GPT-5 replaced GPT-4o as default 7 Aug 2025 (restored for paid users after backlash); GPT-4o retired from ChatGPT 13 Feb 2026 (announced 29 Jan 2026); first GPT-4o API snapshot shut down 23 Oct 2026.
- **Evidence:** ChatGPT reached 1M users in 5 days and ~100M monthly users within ~2 months of its Nov 2022 launch; OpenAI reported 500M weekly users and a $40B funding round at a $300B valuation in Mar 2025, 800M weekly users in Oct 2025, 2.5B prompts/day in Jul 2025, and 1.2B weekly users at DevDay in Sep 2026. GPT-4o itself was retired from ChatGPT on 13 Feb 2026 after OpenAI said only 0.1% of daily users still chose it, with usage having shifted to GPT-5.2.

### 98. Anthropic Claude / Constitutional AI — Succeeded

### 99. Amazon Bedrock Agents / multi-agent collaboration — Failed

- **Category:** Orchestration/Enterprise
- **What it is:** Amazon Bedrock Agents' multi-agent collaboration is a managed AWS service in which a supervisor agent decomposes complex tasks and delegates them to up to 10 specialized collaborator agents with their own tools, knowledge bases, and guardrails.
- **Key dates:** Bedrock Agents launched November 2023; multi-agent collaboration preview December 3, 2024; GA March 10, 2025; AgentCore GA July 23, 2026; Bedrock Agents Classic closed to new customers and placed in maintenance mode July 30, 2026
- **Evidence:** Multi-agent collaboration reached GA on March 10, 2025 after a December 3, 2024 preview, with a supervisor coordinating up to 10 specialized collaborator agents in Supervisor and Routing modes; AWS cited adopters including Moody's for risk-analysis workflows and Syngenta's Cropwise AI. On July 30, 2026, AWS renamed the service Bedrock Agents Classic, closed it to new customers, put it in maintenance mode with no new features, and froze its model catalog, directing new builds to Amazon Bedrock AgentCore (GA July 23, 2026); existing Classic customers were not shut off and no end-of-life date was announced.
- **Why:** It shipped and reached GA, but AWS superseded its console-first, fixed-orchestration model with AgentCore, a framework-agnostic runtime where developers bring their own agent code (Strands, LangGraph, CrewAI) and AWS supplies runtime, memory, gateway, identity, and observability. The Classic service's frozen model catalog after July 30, 2026 made staying on it progressively untenable for new models, so the root cause was architectural rigidity and platform economics: a managed loop could not keep pace with open frameworks and new foundation models. Existing Classic workloads were left running in maintenance mode rather than being shut down outright.
- **Relevance to HIVE:** Copy the supervisor/collaborator specialization, per-agent tools/guardrails, and observability pattern, but avoid depending on a vendor-owned managed orchestration loop that can be frozen and superseded — HIVE should own its orchestration loop and treat the platform as runtime, memory, and identity only.
