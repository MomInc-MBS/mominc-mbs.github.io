# Domain signals and learned communication codes

- **Observed precedent:** Foerster and colleagues studied learned communication protocols in constrained cooperative multi-agent tasks, including differentiable communication during centralized training.
- **Software translation — proposal:** Define separate control, task, evidence, and teaching messages. Compare natural language with typed schemas, domain-native objects, and learned latent codes. Version encoders and translators so message meaning can be traced across changes.
- **Competing branches:** Shared human-readable language; typed symbolic messages; tensors or scene graphs; learned communication; translator specialists. Preserve all as candidates for different domains rather than requiring one universal language.
- **Falsifiable experiment:** Replace a participant, change an encoder version, withhold a task, and drop or alter messages. Measure final task success, decoding cost, error detection, and recovery. Compact codes lose their efficiency claim if interoperability repairs outweigh their transmission savings.
- **Analogy limit:** Independently trained models do not automatically share meaningful hidden representations. Private codes do not provide memory isolation or establish truth.
- **Adjacent avenues:** Type systems, representation alignment, communication theory, protocol negotiation, and multi-agent learning.
