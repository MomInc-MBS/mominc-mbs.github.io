Printed pneumatic robots and structure doing computation

  • Observed mechanism: Gepner and colleagues describe a fully printed soft robot with embedded fluidic control and a standardized fabrication platform.
  • Software translation — proposal: Let structure carry repeated work: arithmetic through a calculator, dependencies through a graph, valid configurations through constraints, and stable procedures through finite-state or compiled workflows. A model selects and adapts these mechanisms.
  • Competing branches: General-model reasoning at every step; a specialist model; deterministic structure; a hybrid planner selecting structures. Keep physical morphology research as its own future branch, linked to the software experiment.
  • Falsifiable experiment: Hold task success criteria constant while changing where the work happens. Measure latency, failure rate, maintenance effort, and adaptation after requirements change. Structural simplification loses support if its apparent speed requires brittle assumptions that fail the changed task.
  • Analogy limit: Fluidic mechanics and software representations are different mechanisms. This paper is a verified physical example; the user's earlier articulated inflatable-limb reference remains separately unidentified.
  • Adjacent avenues: Morphological computation, fluidic logic, constraint programming, compilation, and embodied control.
