MCKINSEY, 2017

UP TO 800 MILLION JOBS, WORLDWIDE

In 2017, McKinsey guessed at a range. By 2030, 400 to 800 million people worldwide could lose work to machines. The 800 million is the top of that range. It is the fastest case, not the likely one. It came out years before generative AI.

McKinsey Global Institute, “Jobs Lost, Jobs Gained: Workforce Transitions in a Time of Automation,” November 2017. 800M is that report's rapid-adoption upper scenario; its midpoint scenario is 400M.

A WORLD NUMBER IS NOT YOUR NUMBER.

800 million is every job, everywhere, added up. It shows how big the problem is. It does not tell you about the one job in this building. Remember how big the claim is.

MCKINSEY, 2023

710,000 FEWER ADMINISTRATIVE ASSISTANTS

McKinsey studied generative AI and work in America in 2023. It said about 710,000 fewer assistant jobs could be needed by 2030. AI takes over the same boring office tasks.

McKinsey & Company, “Generative AI and the future of work in America,” July 2023. A projection to 2030, not a count of jobs already gone.

A JOB NAME IS EASIER TO CHECK THAN A TREND.

“Assistants, down 710,000” can be checked against real hiring data for one job. “AI is coming for office jobs” cannot be checked at all. Trust the number with a job name on it.

MCKINSEY, 2023

THE MOST REPETITIVE WORK GOES FIRST

McKinsey found in 2023 that office support jobs have many repeat tasks. Much of it is moving data around. Machines do that kind of work best.

McKinsey & Company, “Generative AI and the future of work in America,” July 2023.

REPEAT TASKS MATTER, NOT JOB NAMES.

What matters is the kind of task, not the name of the job. A repeat task in a safe-sounding job is still a repeat task. Look at what your day is really made of.
