# Mom's Misinformation Machine: content sourcing starter

Research checked: 2026-10-14. This is a twelve-subject draft catalog and a reusable research structure, not a claim to contain every conspiracy theory. No app is built or deployed by these files. Every card remains `draft` / `pending_human_edit`; the database deliberately contains zero eligible scored questions.

## Use an original iceberg

An iceberg can organize subject familiarity and increasingly specialized knowledge. It is not evidence of truth, a consensus taxonomy, or an objective measurement of rarity. The proposed 1-8 scale is our own editorial framework. This starter occupies tiers 1-5 and does not pretend to cover the deepest tiers. Do not rank tragedy severity, victim counts, or unsupportedness as knowledge depth.

Community charts can suggest candidate names. Independently investigate those candidates and write our own questions and taxonomy. No verified blanket commercial reuse license was established for IcebergCharts in this research, and its main domain returned a fetch error; no chart was copied into this starter. Do not use community screenshots as a production truth database.

## Acquisition pipeline

1. Discover candidate entities, aliases, dates, categories, and reference URLs through Wikidata. Its structured namespaces are [CC0](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Licensing). Wikidata supplies a discovery backbone, not an automatic truth verdict.
2. Use [targeted SPARQL queries for narrow requests and dumps for large extraction](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Help:Data_access). Cache the research locally; do not perform broad public SPARQL requests during gameplay.
3. Verify the exact proposition using primary records, court findings, official investigations, or appropriate scientific institutions. A government file recording an allegation is evidence that someone made the allegation, not necessarily evidence that it occurred.
4. Write original questions and brief explanations, retaining the source URL, publisher, retrieval date, and a document-page locator when relevant. The seed has subject-level/source-page verification; document-level citations and a second source are review tasks for more detailed cards.
5. Review ambiguity, answer aliases, content framing, evidence status, source rights, and proposed tier. Only a human-reviewed claim and matching question may become eligible for scored rounds. Keep review history and question versions.
6. Recheck cards when sources or findings change. Retire obsolete question versions instead of silently changing the meaning of recorded answer statistics.

## Grade a statement, not a topic

"Conspiracy theory or real-world tragedy?" is not a mutually exclusive distinction. Documented covert programs exist. Real events can also attract unsupported conspiracy claims. Keep separate records for the event and each claim about it.

The catalog uses `documented`, `unsupported`, `contradicted`, `disputed`, and `unresolved`. These statuses apply only to `exact_statement`. `historical_conspiracy` is a separate descriptive field; it does not mean all claims connected to a topic are true. The two unsupported or contradicted starter statements are the chemtrails population-control claim and the dated Nibiru collision prediction. Ten other statements are documented. This ratio is a research convenience and is not the intended production deck balance.

The first proposed interaction asks the player to identify a subject. The second displays the exact statement being classified. For a simple two-button presentation, documented statements may map to "Documented" and unsupported/contradicted statements to "Unsupported claim", while the explanation retains the more precise distinction. Disputed and unresolved cards are excluded from binary damage scoring. Reveals should explain and link the evidence.

Answer rarity must be based on valid answers to the same question/version. These examples each identify one intended subject and cannot reproduce a broad survey-answer rarity mechanic on their own. Before shipping, author open-ended questions with multiple independently valid answers, define what counts as an attempt and a valid answer, collect enough player data, specify cold-start behavior, and version calibration. Until then, the seed explicitly assigns no propulsion values. Never award extra propulsion for endorsing an unsupported statement or naming a perpetrator with a larger victim count.

## Rights and provenance

- [Wikidata structured data is CC0](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Licensing). The twelve reviewed-source draft cards do not depend on guessed Wikidata IDs. A separate bounded CC0 discovery export now contains 400 unique candidate entities: 200 conspiracy theories, 100 human serial-killer occupation matches, and 100 massacres. Those imported entity classifications are unreviewed discovery metadata, not evidence verdicts.
- Wikimedia prose generally uses CC BY-SA 4.0; Commons media has item-specific licenses. If later reusing such material, preserve attribution, revision/source information, the applicable license, and notices of adaptation. See [Wikimedia's licensing guidance](https://attribution.wikimedia.org/attribution-signals/license.html). Do not assume text and image licenses match.
- Works created by federal employees as official duties generally do not receive US copyright protection. A federal-hosted file can still contain third-party copyrighted material. Treat item-level rights as a separate check before copying or distributing assets. See the [US Copyright Office compendium](https://www.copyright.gov/comp3/docs/compendium.pdf).
- The [Museum's terms](https://www.ushmm.org/copyright-and-legal-information/terms-of-use) specify item-level restrictions and prohibit using its content to develop AI systems without written consent. The Museum is a linked factual verification source here, not a corpus for ingestion, embeddings, training, or copied production prose. No Museum media is included. Future asset reuse needs a separate rights review.
- No blanket permissive reuse license was established for ECCC website content in this research. The starter uses a link and independently worded account of a judicial finding; future copying of documents or images requires its own rights review.

Keep satire directed at MOM's manipulation and the player's escape predicament. Precise factual wording makes the classification fair. Frame documented tragedies and atrocities without invented quotes, denial, victim jokes, or perpetrator glorification; avoid turning a contested personal death toll into a leaderboard statistic.

## Files and local verification

`seed-catalog.json` is the reviewable source. `schema.sql` normalizes subjects, aliases, claims, sources, questions, accepted answers, relations, review history, and versioned answer statistics. `eligible_questions` filters for approved matching claim/question classifications. The ingestion script refuses to overwrite a populated catalog unless `--overwrite` is explicitly passed, and leaves all player statistics empty.

Run from this directory:

```powershell
python .\import_seed.py
```

The command builds `starter-catalog.sqlite3`, reads back row counts, runs `PRAGMA foreign_key_check`, and checks database integrity. Use `--database PATH` for a separate import. Use `--overwrite` only to deliberately rebuild the existing research starter; its catalog rows are replaced in a transaction. This is a local research starter, not a live service or deployment.


## Actual bounded Wikidata export

The `candidates/` directory includes raw responses, normalized JSON, exact SPARQL files, repeatable fetch scripts, and `manifest.json` with request URLs, retrieval times, SHA-256 digests, licensing, and coverage notes. Class IDs were checked against Wikidata labels through its entity API: Q159535 (conspiracy theory), Q484188 (serial killer), and Q3199915 (massacre).

The conspiracy SPARQL export returned 200 entities. The serial-killer and massacre SPARQL requests returned HTTP 429 with an explicit active-outage limit of one request per minute. No WDQS retry was attempted. A bounded Wikidata API relation-search fallback then returned 100 direct serial-killer occupation matches filtered to human instances, plus 100 direct massacre instances. The fallback has narrower coverage than subclass-expanding SPARQL; search indexing may lag edits. An initial serial-killer search included fictional characters, so the final export adds the human-instance filter. The delivered serial-killer candidate file is the filtered result.

All 400 rows have distinct Wikidata IDs in this snapshot. This is a limited sample with selection/indexing bias, not a complete population, objective iceberg, verified perpetrators list, or approved question bank. Missing English labels remain IDs. Neither entity membership nor an English label establishes a claim as true. New cards still need source verification and human editing, especially allegations concerning living people.

`import_candidates.py` imports these records into separate `discovery_candidates`, `discovery_exports`, and membership tables. It does not create claims or questions. Run `python .\import_candidates.py` after the seed import. Readback: 400 distinct candidate entities and 400 membership rows; 12 draft questions; zero eligible scored questions; foreign-key check empty and integrity check `ok`.
